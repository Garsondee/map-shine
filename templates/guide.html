What is Map Shine?

Map Shine is designed to empower Game Masters and map-makers with a suite of powerful visual effects that are intuitive and easy to implement. The core philosophy is to handle the complex technical work behind the scenes, allowing you to focus on the creative process. From shimmering metallic surfaces and dynamic cloud shadows to magical glowing runes and crackling fire, this module aims to be your one-stop shop for bringing your scenes to life.

The Core Concept: Automatic Effects via "Effect Maps"

The magic of Map Shine lies in its automatic effect system. You don't need to configure complex settings for every tile or draw special lighting. Instead, the module automatically detects and applies effects based on the filenames of your textures.

This is done using special texture files called Effect Maps. An Effect Map is simply a secondary image that you place in the same folder as your main map texture. You give it the same name as your main map, but with a special suffix. For example:

  • Your main map: MyDungeon.webp
  • The Effect Map for metallic shine: MyDungeon_Specular.webp

When you load MyDungeon.webp into a scene (either as the background or a tile), Map Shine sees the _Specular file and automatically applies the Metallic Shine effect to it. The same principle applies to dozens of other effects!

Getting Started: Your First Effect (Metallic Shine)

Let's walk through adding a simple but impressive effect: making the metal parts of a treasure chest shine.

Step 1: The Base Map

Start with your regular map image. Here, we have a simple treasure chest on a wooden floor.

Step 2: Create the Specular Map

In an image editor (like Photoshop, GIMP, or Krita), create a new black and white image. This is your Specular Map. The rule is simple:

  • White areas will be shiny.
  • Black areas will be dull.
  • Shades of grey will have partial shininess.

For our chest, we'll paint the metal parts white and leave everything else black.

Step 3: Naming and Placement

Save both files in the same folder with the correct names:

  • TreasureChest.webp
  • TreasureChest_Specular.webp

Step 4: See the Magic!

Create a tile in your Foundry scene using TreasureChest.webp. The Metallic Shine effect will automatically appear!

Step 5: Tweak and Customize

Open the Map Shine Editor from the token controls. Find the "Metallic Shine" section and experiment with the settings to change the color, speed, and intensity of the effect until it's perfect.

The Editor Panel: Your Control Center

The editor gives you granular control over every effect. It's divided into a few key areas:

  • Main Controls & Profiles: At the top, you can manage settings that affect the entire module. This is where you save and load different looks for your scenes.
  • Post-Processing Pipeline: This column on the left contains effects that are applied to the entire screen, like cinematic color grading, vignettes, and lens effects.
  • Main Effects Area: The main columns contain individual settings for all the map-based effects like Metallic Shine, Cloud Shadows, and Fire Particles.

Scene Appearances vs. World Profiles

Map Shine features a powerful two-tier system for saving your settings. In the "Main Controls" section, you'll see options for both.

  • Scene Appearances: These are visual profiles saved *directly with the scene*. They allow you to create and switch between multiple, unique looks for a single map (e.g., a "Day" version and a "Night" version). Use the "Activate for All" button to push a selected appearance to all players.
  • World Profile Library: These are reusable presets saved at the *world level*. You can save a look you love—like a perfect "Eerie Undead" color grade—and easily load it into any scene in your world as a starting point.

The Day/Night Clock

Accessible from the token controls, the Day/Night Clock is your tool for controlling all time-based effects. Drag the sun/moon icon around the dial to change the time. As you do, you'll see the scene's lighting and shadows change in real-time. This clock controls:

  • Time of Day Color Grade: The overall color, brightness, and mood of outdoor areas.
  • Building Shadows: The length and direction of shadows cast by buildings.

A Tour of Key Effects (Texture-Based)

Here are some of the most common effect types and the maps they require:

Reflective & Wet Surfaces

Requires: _Specular.webp

The core shine effect. Use it for polished metal, wet cavern floors, icy surfaces, or anything that should reflect light.

Shadows

Requires: _Outdoors.webp, _Canopy.webp, _Structural.webp

Map Shine has a powerful shadow system. Use _Outdoors to define what's inside vs. outside, _Canopy for dappled forest light, and _Structural for indoor shadows from things like rafters or pillars.

Particles & Fire

Requires: _Fire.webp, _Sparks.webp, _Dust.webp

Bring your maps to life with animated particles. The _Fire map generates flames and embers, _Sparks creates energetic sparks, and _Dust adds subtle motes floating in sunbeams.

Glows & Emissives

Requires: _Ambient.webp, _GroundGlow.webp

Make parts of your map glow! _Ambient is a general-purpose glow for things like lava or magic runes. _GroundGlow is a special effect that only appears in dark, unlit areas.

The Complete Water System

Requires: _Water.webp (and optionally _Shoreline.webp)

This is a multi-layered effect for creating realistic and dynamic water. Start with a `_Water.webp` map where white marks your water areas. The system will then add:

  • Wave Distortion: An underlying ripple that distorts the view of the seabed.
  • Underwater Caustics: Shifting patterns of light on the ground beneath the water.
  • Surface Foam & Sheen: Animated foam and light reflections on the water's surface.
  • Shoreline Effects: If you provide a soft, grayscale `_Shoreline.webp` map (white where the foam should be thickest), the module will generate animated foam and spray along the coast. If you don't provide one, it will attempt to auto-detect the shoreline.

Magical & Environmental Effects

Requires: _Iridescence.webp, _Prism.webp, _Heat.webp

These maps are for more fantastical or specific environmental effects.

  • Iridescence: Creates a shimmering, oil-slick or soap-bubble effect. Perfect for fey crossings or magical potions.
  • Prism: Splits the light in bright areas, creating a rainbow fringe. Great for crystals or divine magic.
  • Heat Distortion: Creates a rising heat-haze that distorts the view. Essential for fire, forges, and desert scenes.

Advanced Tools: The Map Points Editor

Some effects, like lightning or a swarm of flies, aren't tied to a specific texture. For these, you use the Map Points Editor, which lets you draw points, lines, and shapes directly onto the map and assign an effect to them.

Example 1: Creating Lightning

  1. Open the Map Points Editor from the Editor Panel.
  2. Create a new "Line" group and give it a name.
  3. Check the "Use as Effect Source" box and select "Lightning" from the "Target Effect" dropdown.
  4. Activate placement mode and click two points on the map.

A lightning bolt will now arc between those points!

Example 2: Creating a Fly Swarm

The **Smelly Flies** effect uses an Area group to define where flies can land and walk around, and a Point group to define where they swarm in the air.

  1. Create a new "Area" group. Check "Use as Effect Source" and select "Smelly Flies".
  2. Activate placement mode and click to draw a polygon around the area where flies can land (e.g., a pile of rubbish).
  3. The flies will now swarm near the center of this area and land within its bounds.

Deep Dive: The Post-Processing Pipeline

Located in the left-hand column of the Editor, the Post-Processing Pipeline contains effects that apply to the entire screen, allowing you to create a truly cinematic look for your scenes. This is where you can fine-tune the final mood and atmosphere.

Color Correction & Grading

This is the heart of the pipeline. You can use the sliders to adjust Brightness, Contrast, and Saturation, or dive deeper with professional tools:

  • Color Presets: Quickly apply a look like "Cinematic" or "Vintage Film". You can also save your own custom color grades as "Favorites" to reuse later.
  • Curves Editor: For precise, non-linear control over the tonal range of your scene, just like in Photoshop.
  • Selective Color: Isolate a specific color in your scene and change it. For example, you could make everything black and white except for the color red.
  • LUTs (Look-Up Tables): Advanced users can apply professional `.cube` LUT files for industry-standard color grading.

Lens & Film Effects

Simulate real-world camera effects to add a touch of realism or style:

  • Vignette: Darkens the corners of the screen to draw focus to the center.
  • Chromatic Aberration: Creates a subtle color fringe effect, often seen at the edges of camera lenses.
  • Film Grain: Adds a procedural film grain or digital noise effect over the final image for a gritty, cinematic feel.

Automated & Scene-Wide Effects

Some effects are not tied to a specific map texture but happen automatically in response to game events. You can configure these in the main effects area of the editor.

  • Pause & Combat Effects: Apply a configurable visual transition when the game is paused or when combat starts. You can desaturate the scene, slow down all animations, or apply a custom color tint to signal a change in game state.
  • Dynamic Exposure ("Dazzle"): Creates a "dazzle" effect when a token moves from a dark indoor area (as defined by your `_Outdoors` map) to a bright outdoor one, simulating eyes adjusting to bright light.

Troubleshooting & Diagnostics

If an effect isn't showing up, here are the first things to check:

  1. Check your filenames! This is the most common issue. Ensure the suffix is correct (e.g., _Specular, not _specular) and that there are no typos.
  2. Check the Editor Panel. Is the master "Enable All Effects" toggle on? Is the specific effect you want enabled?
  3. Use the Diagnostic Tools. In the Editor Panel, under "World Profiles and Diagnostics", check the "Show Discovered Masks" box. This will overlay your map with colors, showing you exactly which effect maps Map Shine has found and where they are being applied. It's the best way to confirm the module can see your files.
